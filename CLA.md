# Contributor Licence Agreement — Varve

**Varve is not yet accepting external contributions.** This document
describes the CLA process we intend to use once the project opens to
outside contributors, so the plan is transparent in advance. Do not sign
anything yet — see [ICLA.md](ICLA.md) and [CCLA.md](CCLA.md), which are
still drafts awaiting legal review and are explicitly not in effect.

Thank you for considering a future contribution to Varve. This document
explains why the project asks contributors to sign a Contributor Licence
Agreement (CLA), what rights you grant, and what rights you retain.

## Why a CLA?

Varve is licensed under the **Functional Source License, Version 1.1, MIT
Future License (FSL-1.1-MIT)** — a source-available licence that permits
free use for internal operations, personal projects, and plugin development,
but restricts competing commercial redistribution and hosted-service
offerings. After two years each version converts to MIT.

The project owner intends to offer a **future Pro edition** under a different
licensing model (subscription or proprietary). Without a CLA, the project
owner could not include community contributions in a proprietary edition
without obtaining separate permission from each contributor. The CLA grants
the necessary rights in advance so that contributions can be used across all
current and future editions while **you keep full copyright ownership** of
your work.

## What the CLA does

- **You retain copyright.** The CLA is a licence grant, not a copyright
  assignment. You own your contribution and may use it elsewhere.
- **You grant a broad, perpetual licence** to the project owner to use,
  modify, distribute, sublicense, and relicense your contribution as part
  of the Varve project under any licence.
- **You represent that you have the right** to make the contribution and
  that it does not infringe anyone else's rights.
- **You are not required to provide support** or future updates for your
  contribution.

## Individual vs. Corporate CLA

- **Individual CLA (ICLA):** For contributions you make on your own time
  and your own behalf.
- **Corporate CLA (CCLA):** If your contribution is made as part of your
  job or using employer resources, your employer must sign a CCLA. You
  as the contributor also confirm your authorisation through the signing
  process.

## DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin)

In addition to the CLA, all commits must include a `Signed-off-by:` trailer
(the DCO). The DCO certifies that you have the right to submit the work
under the project's licence. The combination of CLA + DCO provides two
independent layers of provenance:

| Layer | Purpose | Method |
|-------|---------|--------|
| **CLA** | Grants relicensing rights for all editions | One-time online signing |
| **DCO** | Certifies you authored the work | `git commit -s` per commit |

## What signing looks like

1. Open a pull request.
2. The CLA Assistant bot posts a comment with a link to the agreement.
3. Click the link, review the CLA, and sign via your GitHub account.
4. The bot updates the PR status to passing.
5. Future PRs recognise your prior signature automatically.
6. Each commit must also carry a `Signed-off-by:` line (`git commit -s`).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I lose copyright ownership of my contribution?**
No. The CLA is a licence, not an assignment. You keep full ownership.

**Can I use my contribution in my own projects?**
Yes. The CLA does not restrict your use of your own code.

**What if my employer owns my work?**
Ask your employer to sign a Corporate CLA (CCLA.md). You can then contribute
as an authorised representative.

**What if I contributed without employer approval?**
Contact the project owner immediately at the email associated with the
K-Arthur GitHub account. The situation will be reviewed case by case.

**What if I change my GitHub username?**
The CLA signing is tied to your GitHub account. If you change your username,
contact the project owner to update the signature record.

**What information is stored when I sign?**
Your GitHub username, the date and time of signing, the agreement version,
and any information you provide in custom fields. This record is stored in
the repository's signature file and is visible to maintainers.

**Can I revoke my CLA?**
The CLA is perpetual and irrevocable for contributions already accepted.
You may stop contributing at any time.

## Legal review

These documents are **drafts** provided for review. Neither the ICLA nor the
CCLA is final until approved by qualified legal counsel. If you have
questions about the legal effect of these agreements, consult your own
attorney.
